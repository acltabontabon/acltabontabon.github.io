Vortex is a local-first performance engineering workbench. Point it at your service's OpenAPI spec, describe the
traffic you're worried about, and it turns that into a real k6 workload, runs it on your machine, and hands back
evidence — not terminal noise.

[![CI](https://github.com/acltabontabon/vortex/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/acltabontabon/vortex/actions/workflows/ci.yml)
[![License: Apache 2.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-Apache%202.0-blue.svg)](https://github.com/acltabontabon/vortex/blob/main/LICENSE)

## THE PROBLEM

Running a load test isn't hard — k6 already does that extremely well. The hard part is everything else: what traffic
your service actually receives, what "normal" vs "peak" actually look like, whether 50 req/s or 5,000 is the number
worth testing, and whether it was your service that failed or your load generator. Most teams answer this by jumping
between an observability dashboard, API specs, terminal output, and memory — and the honest answer that survives
that process is often "probably enough." That's not evidence, that's a shrug with a chart. Vortex turns that chain
into one coherent workflow.

---

## FEATURES

- Parses your OpenAPI spec deterministically instead of hand-rolling boilerplate
- Models production-realistic traffic: weighted operation mixes, distinct arrival rate and concurrency, optional
  calibration from real Prometheus or Dynatrace data
- Runs real k6 locally, with a preflight showing exactly what you're about to send and a typed confirmation before
  hitting any non-local target
- Returns a deterministic pass/fail verdict against your latency and error objectives — which resource ran out
  first, and whether the run was even valid
- Keeps execution history, capacity trends, and comparisons that only run when two tests actually measured the
  same thing
- Optional Ollama-backed assistant explains findings Vortex computed — it cannot invent numbers

---

## WHY LOCAL-FIRST

Vortex runs on your machine, against your service, before you provision expensive test infrastructure — that's the
entire point, not a limitation. A laptop isn't production, and Vortex will never claim a local run proves you'll
handle Black Friday. What it will do is replace guessing with evidence before you spend money proving it: whether
your service behaves under load, roughly where it struggles, what's likely to break first, and whether this build
regressed.

Vortex is not another load generator (k6 already does that), not an APM (Prometheus/Dynatrace stay your
observability platform), and not a production-capacity oracle.

---

## STATUS

Early alpha. The core workflow — import, model, run, evaluate, compare — works end to end with real k6 and real
services. Production-informed calibration against Prometheus and Dynatrace is working. Configuration, APIs, and UX
are still expected to change.
